## The stance

An hour of reading wants what print figured out centuries ago: a warm ground that doesn't glare, a text face with real contrast between strokes, and a layout rhythm measured in ems of body text rather than pixels of chrome. Hence the vocabulary the [[design-tokens|token layer]] is written in: papers (纸 the sheet, 页 the page, 素 the plain ground, 帛 the silk) instead of gray-50 through gray-200; inks and pigments from a painter's box — 朱 wine, 石 burnt orange, 赭 ochre, 黛 teal, 紫 violet — instead of primary/secondary/accent. The names are not decoration: they keep every new color decision anchored to the same physical metaphor, which is why five different surfaces still read as one sheet of paper.

## Two grounds, because two kinds of page

A wiki is read two ways. You **read** a note — one column, an hour, the eyes want calm: a slightly cool paper, a neutral ink, one accent for links and numbers. You **browse** a shelf — a grid of cards, a glance, a choice: the paper can be warmer, the titles can be a display serif, the accent can be a deep wine that reads as a mark rather than a link. Giving those two situations one palette would compromise both. So the semantic tier has two contexts — the reading column (`--color-*`) and the browse shelf (`--wb-*`) — drawn from one box of pigments, and a page declares which one it is. The result is a site that feels like one object and two rooms.

## Dark is a second edition, not an inversion

Algorithmic dark modes invert lightness and call it done; text becomes chalk on slate and every tinted surface turns to mud. Here the dark theme is a **second hand-set edition**: dusk grounds and moon-white inks chosen on their own, accent pigments re-brightened just enough to hold their AA contrast on the darker papers. It costs a second set of decisions — that is the point. The switch honors the reader's explicit choice only ([[design-tokens#light-is-the-identity|never the OS preference]]), because a page that flips its own theme mid-read is a page that startles.

## CJK is load-bearing

Mixed Chinese/Latin text is where most themes quietly fall apart: emphasis that refuses to close against a full-width period, code diagrams that shear because a Hanzi is 1.7 cells wide. Both are treated here as correctness bugs, not typographic taste — the [[kitchen-sink#inline-marks|dialect parses CJK emphasis correctly]], and the code font subset pins a Hanzi to exactly two cells so box-drawing survives translation. A theme that only works in English is half a theme.
