## Small notes, linked densely

A garden note earns its keep by being small enough to finish and linked well enough to be found. Prefer five linked notes to one sprawling page; let [[inkbrush/wikilinks|wikilinks]] carry the structure instead of a table of contents. Dead links are part of the method — each one is a note you owe yourself.

## Pruning

Old notes rot in two ways: they become wrong, or they become orphans. Wrongness is the easy case — edit the offending block where you see it, and the journal remembers what it used to say ([[inkbrush/revisions|revision history]] has the details). Orphans need pruning: link them in, merge them down, or delete them without guilt. A garden is judged by what it keeps alive, not by what it has accumulated.

## Let the tools carry the ceremony

Commits, history, backups — the ceremony is real, but it shouldn't be yours. With `autocommit` on, saves become git commits on their own; imports arrive from the [[inkbrush/inbox|inbox]] on their own; even translations are one click in [[inkbrush/ai-assist|AI assist]] — this garden's eighteen editions say how far that goes. What's left for you is the only part that was ever interesting: having something to say.
